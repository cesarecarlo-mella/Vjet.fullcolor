\paragraph{Setup.}
Every finite remainder is a sum $\sum q\,R_r(y,z)\,T_g\,c_m$ of rational functions, constants and 579 pure functions
$T_g$. The $T_g$ are iterated integrals with base point $(x,y)=(0,0)$, where every non-empty integral vanishes after
shuffle regularisation. They satisfy a closed canonical system $dF=\sum_k d\log W_k\,M_k F$ of 503 functions, which
we integrate numerically.

\paragraph{Boundary values (computed once).}
The origin is singular, so we do not start there. We expand in series instead (see the figure):
\begin{enumerate}
\item along the edge $x=0$, from $(0,0)$ to $(0,\tfrac12)$, as a series in $y$. On the edge only $y=0,1$ are
  singular, and the regularisation sets $\log y\to0$ at the origin;
\item off the edge at fixed $y=\tfrac12$, as a series in $\sqrt{x}$ (the square-root letters go like $\sqrt x$), up to
  $S=(0.02,\tfrac12)$. Here $\log x\to0$ at $x=0$;
\item one numerical transport (Bulirsch--Stoer) from $S$ to the central point $P_0=(0.3,0.3)$.
\end{enumerate}
The values at $P_0$ are stored once and for all and shipped with the evaluator. The mirror construction, through
$(\tfrac12,0)$ and $(\tfrac12,0.02)$, gives an independent set, used only for the error estimate.

\paragraph{Evaluation at a point $P$.}
We integrate the system from $P_0$ to $P$ along a straight path, deformed into the complex plane
($z(t)=P_0+(t+4i\delta\,t(1-t))(P-P_0)$). Three refinements:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Spurious letters.} Six letters vanish on curves inside the triangle. The amplitude is smooth there, but
  the equations are not. The deformation $\delta$ is chosen so that the complex path stays away from their zeros.
\item \emph{Points on a spurious curve.} Such a point is replaced by the symmetric average of $P\pm h\,n$,
  $P\pm 2h\,n$, $P\pm 3h\,n$ across the curve ($n$ its normal), which is accurate to $O(h^6)$.
\item \emph{Error estimate.} Each point is evaluated a second time, from the mirror boundary values and on another
  path; the estimate is ten times the difference.
\end{itemize}
In the bulk the accuracy is about $10^{-12}$, and about $10^{-13}$ against the exact planar results. Near the edges
double precision loses digits, which the error estimate reports; there the corner expansions take over.

\section*{High-precision values at $P_0$ (27 digits)}

\paragraph{1. A double series at the corner.}
Here we use the exact system: 586 functions in an integral basis, with integer matrices $M_k$
(\texttt{out/basis.wl}, \texttt{out/deq.wl}); the 579 $T_g$ are integer combinations of them. Near $(0,0)$ every function is
a double series
\[
  F(x,y)=\sum c\;x^{a/2}\,y^{b/2}\,\log^k x\,\log^l y ,\qquad c\ \text{exact rational (times $1$ or $i$)}.
\]
We obtain it from the blow-up series of the ITER family: chart 1 ($x=t\,y^2$) and its mirror chart 4 ($y=t\,x^2$),
each to order $t^{16}$ and $B^{48}$ ($B$ the other variable). We undo the blow-up: $t=x/y^2$, $\log t=\log x-2\log y$.
\begin{itemize}
\item No negative powers appear, and the two charts agree exactly on all $1.3\cdot10^6$ terms they share. Several
  letters vanish at the corner ($x+y$, $x-y+y^2$, $(x+y)^2-x$, \dots), but the functions do not see them there: the
  blow-up is needed for the rational functions only.
\item The union of the two charts holds every term of total degree $\tfrac{a+b}2\le 32$ (the first missing one is
  $x^{16}y^{17}$). The truncation error is about $(|x|+|y|)^{33}$: $10^{-34}$ at $(0.05,0.05)$, $10^{-27}$ at
  $(0.08,0.08)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. Two start points,} evaluated from the series at 40 digits:
\[
  S_A=(0.05,\,0.05),\qquad S_B=(0.04,\,0.06).
\]

\paragraph{3. Transport to $P_0$ in quad precision.}
From each start point we integrate to $P_0=(0.3,0.3)$ along its own complex-deformed straight path,
\[
  x(t)=S_x+\bigl(t+4i\,\delta_x\,t(1-t)\bigr)(0.3-S_x),\qquad
  y(t)=S_y+\bigl(t+4i\,\delta_y\,t(1-t)\bigr)(0.3-S_y),\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]
with $\delta_A=(-0.15,\,-0.25)$ (below the real plane) and $\delta_B=(0.1,\,0.2)$ (above). The root
$r=\sqrt{xyz}$ is integrated along ($r>0$ at $S$). The integrator is Bulirsch--Stoer (modified midpoint, up to 16
extrapolation levels) in double-double arithmetic ($\approx32$ digits), with tolerance $10^{-32}$ relative to the largest
function. Each path takes about one minute (\texttt{cpp/iter\_quad}, \texttt{quad\_data.py}).

\paragraph{4. Result.}
The two determinations agree to $3\cdot10^{-28}$ relative to the largest function ($4.5\cdot10^4$), i.e.\ 27--28 digits.
Further checks, all within $2\cdot10^{-27}$: a second path from $S_A$ ($\delta=(0.1,0.2)$), and start points $(0.06,0.06)$,
$(0.06,0.04)$, $(0.08,0.08)$; the last differs by its series truncation, $2\cdot10^{-27}$. The average is stored in
\texttt{out/iter\_quad\_P0.bin} (input of \texttt{cpp/iter\_quad}) and \texttt{out/P0\_values\_quad.m} (30 digits). The values
used so far by the double evaluator (edge construction above) are off by up to $5\cdot10^{-8}$ in absolute terms
($10^{-12}$ of the largest generator; median $10^{-13}$ relative).

Two technical points: each half of a double-double number must be read back as a double (reading the decimal
string at higher precision costs a factor $10^{15}$), and the extrapolation weights $n_{k-j}^2/(n_k^2-n_{k-j}^2)$ must
be exact in double-double (in double they cap the accuracy at $10^{-24}$).

\paragraph{5. Into the double evaluator.} The quad values replace the start values of the double evaluator. The two
systems are two bases of the same 503-dimensional space (weights $0,\dots,6$: $1, 5, 24, 75, 113, 261, 24$):
\begin{itemize}
\item the \textbf{503 system} (double): an orthonormal basis, floating-point matrices with entries $|A|\le1$;
\item the \textbf{586 system} (series, quad): integer matrices. Weights $0$--$4$ and $6$ have a true integral basis with the
  same counts. At weight 5 it keeps a redundant spanning set of 344 functions for 261 dimensions: the 247 generators
  and 97 derivatives of the weight-6 generators. A true integral basis there would need huge integers.
\end{itemize}
The 503 states are exact rational combinations of the 586 functions (\texttt{quad\_p0\_to\_double.py}). With the quad
start values, the two evaluations of the error estimate share their start, so they differ only by the path. The
estimate adds a term $\epsilon\sum|\text{terms}|$ for the systematic error of the double system, which is the same on
every path.

\section*{Production regions}
Each production region $A>1$ has its own triangle $(X,Y)$, $Z=1-X-Y=1/A$, and its own hub $(0.3,0.3)$
(\texttt{notes/continuation\_notes.tex}). Its start values $P_0$ are defined exactly as in the decay:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The corner series, continued.} The region's corner $X,Y\to0$ (threshold) is a decay corner, and its two
  small variables are $(-X/Z,\,-Y/Z)$. Both cross zero from above (Feynman prescription): $\log s\to\log|s|+i\pi$,
  $\sqrt s\to i\sqrt{|s|}$ for each, so that $r\to-\sqrt{|xyz|}$.
\item \textbf{Two start points, two quad paths} to the hub, with the region's integer system $M'_j=\sum_k c_{kj}M_k$.
\end{enumerate}

$x$ & $c_2$: $y,z\to0$ & chart 5: $y=t\,z^2$ & $(0.001,0.2)$, $(0.0005,0.15)$ & $2\cdot10^{-29}$ & $1.9\cdot10^{-27}$ \\
$y$ & $c_3$: $x,z\to0$ & chart 3: $x=t\,z^2$ & $(0.001,0.2)$, $(0.0005,0.15)$ & $5\cdot10^{-30}$ & $2.5\cdot10^{-27}$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Why charts at $c_2$, $c_3$.} There the functions are \emph{not} double series in the two small variables:
  undoing the blow-up leaves negative powers already at weight 1. The letter $1-x=y+z$ (at $c_2$), resp.\ $1-y=x+z$ (at
  $c_3$), vanishes at the corner, and the non-transversal letters are seen. So we use the exact series in one chart.
  It is continued with $\log t\to\log|t|-i\pi$ and $\log B\to\log|B|+i\pi$ ($t=\text{SMALL}/\text{BIG}^2<0$), and
  evaluated at small $|t|\approx0.02$, i.e.\ close to the edge $X=0$. At $c_1$ the functions are a double series, as in
  the decay.
\item \textbf{The series are exact.} The orders are 16 in $t$ and 48 in $B$, with exact boundary constants. In the decay,
  the chart 3 and 5 series agree with a quad integration from $P_0$ to $10^{-27}$.
\item \textbf{Cross-check (not the definition).} A quad integration from the decay $P_0$ straight to the physical image
  of the hub, along a path that respects the prescription, agrees with the hub values to $8\cdot10^{-28}$ (region $z$),
  $8\cdot10^{-28}$ ($x$) and $2\cdot10^{-27}$ ($y$). The earlier double continuation through the corner agrees to
  $10^{-11}$, its own precision.

  \texttt{out/region\_<A>\_iter\_quad.bin} (quad), and the head of \texttt{out/region\_<A>\_bs.bin} (double).
\end{itemize}
